## 1. Introduction to Capstone Project

### What is a Capstone Project?

A **Capstone Project** is a project in which students research a topic, understand the subject deeply and combine their knowledge to develop a solution to a real-world problem.

An **AI Capstone Project** gives students an opportunity to apply their Artificial Intelligence skills and showcase their learning through a practical project.

### Importance of a Capstone Project

A Capstone Project helps students:

1. Apply their theoretical knowledge to real-life problems.
2. Develop problem-solving skills.
3. Work effectively as a team.
4. Identify problems and understand their impact on users.
5. Generate ideas and select suitable solutions.
6. Understand which type of AI can solve a particular problem.
7. Collect and use data ethically.
8. Test prototypes and improve solutions using feedback.
9. Present or pitch their solutions to others.

### Examples of AI Capstone Projects

| Project | Description |
|---|---|
| Stock Price Predictor | Predicts the future prices of stocks using historical data. |
| Sentiment Analyzer | Identifies whether a text expresses positive, negative or neutral feelings. |
| Movie Ticket Price Predictor | Predicts movie ticket prices based on different factors. |
| Student Results Predictor | Predicts students' academic performance. |
| Human Activity Recognition | Recognises activities such as walking, running or sitting using smartphone sensor data. |
| Human and Animal Classification | Identifies whether an image contains a human or an animal. |

## 2. Identifying Patterns Before Starting an AI Project

Before starting an AI project, we must ask a fundamental question:

**"Is there a Pattern?"**

A pattern is a repeated or identifiable relationship in data that can help an AI system learn and make predictions or decisions.

AI uses patterns in data to solve problems.

**Example:**

Suppose we have students' previous examination marks and study hours.

We may observe that students who study for more hours often score higher marks.

An AI system can learn from such patterns to predict students' results.

If there is no useful pattern in the available data, the problem may not be suitable for an AI-based solution.

### Five AI Approaches

Predictive analysis helps identify patterns by asking different types of questions.

| Question | AI Approach | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Which category? | Classification | Assigns data to predefined categories. | Classifying emails as spam or not spam. |
| How much or how many? | Regression | Predicts a numerical value. | Predicting house prices. |
| Which group? | Clustering | Groups similar data together. | Grouping customers based on shopping habits. |
| Is this unusual? | Anomaly Detection | Identifies unusual or abnormal patterns. | Detecting unusual bank transactions. |
| Which option should be taken? | Recommendation | Suggests suitable options. | Recommending movies based on interests. |

### Important Difference

- **Classification:** Predicts a category or class.
- **Regression:** Predicts a numerical value.
- **Clustering:** Groups similar data without predefined categories.
- **Anomaly Detection:** Finds unusual patterns.
- **Recommendation:** Suggests suitable choices.

## 3. Problem Decomposition

### Definition

**Problem Decomposition** is the process of breaking a complex problem into smaller and simpler parts so that it becomes easier to understand and solve.

It helps us solve complicated problems step by step instead of solving everything at once.

### Steps of Problem Decomposition

**Step 1: Understand the Problem**

- Read and understand the problem carefully.
- Restate the problem in your own words.
- Identify the required inputs and outputs.
- Ask questions to clarify doubts.

**Step 2: Break the Problem into Large Parts**

- Divide the main problem into a few major sub-problems.
- Write down these parts separately.

**Step 3: Break Complicated Parts into Smaller Parts**

- Identify difficult sub-problems.
- Divide them further into simpler tasks.
- Continue until the tasks become manageable.

**Step 4: Code One Small Part at a Time**

- Write the code or query for each small part.
- Test each part separately.
- Identify and fix errors.
- Combine the parts to complete the solution.

### Example: Traffic Management Problem

**Main Problem:** People face traffic jams during office hours.

The problem can be divided into the following parts:

1. Bad roads
2. Accidents
3. Office-hour traffic
4. Rash driving
5. Inappropriate traffic signals
6. Overcrowded areas

Rash driving can be further divided into:

- Overspeeding
- New drivers with less experience
- Careless driving

By solving each smaller problem separately, the overall traffic problem becomes easier to manage.

## 4. Critical Thinking and Creative Thinking

### Critical Thinking

**Definition:** Critical Thinking is the ability to analyse a situation and make a judgement based on facts and data.

In simple words, it means thinking logically before making a decision.

**Example:**

A student wants to buy a laptop. They compare its price, features, performance and reviews before making a decision.

This is Critical Thinking.

### Creative Thinking

**Definition:** Creative Thinking is the ability to come up with new ideas or solutions.

In simple words, it means thinking differently and finding innovative ways to solve a problem.

**Example:**

A student develops an application that reminds people to switch off electrical appliances when they are not in use.

This is Creative Thinking.

### Difference Between Critical and Creative Thinking

| Critical Thinking | Creative Thinking |
|---|---|
| Analyses information and facts. | Generates new ideas. |
| Focuses on logical decisions. | Focuses on innovation. |
| Evaluates possible solutions. | Suggests possible solutions. |
| Example: Comparing different solutions. | Example: Developing a new solution. |

### Importance

Both skills help students:

- Ask relevant questions.
- Gather and analyse information.
- Generate ideas.
- Form conclusions.
- Develop effective solutions.
- Become innovators and problem-solvers.

## 5. Design Thinking

### Definition

**Design Thinking** is a non-linear and iterative process used to understand users, challenge assumptions, redefine problems and create innovative solutions through prototyping and testing.

In simple words, Design Thinking is a method of solving problems by understanding the people who are facing them.

### Features of Design Thinking

1. It is user-centred.
2. It focuses on understanding real problems.
3. It encourages creativity and innovation.
4. It involves developing and testing solutions.
5. It is iterative, meaning stages can be repeated to improve the solution.
6. It is useful for complex or unclear problems.

### Five Stages of Design Thinking

1. Empathize
2. Define
3. Ideate
4. Prototype
5. Test

### Example: Developing an Application to Reduce Traffic Problems

| Stage | Activity |
|---|---|
| Empathize | Talk to people who face traffic jams daily. |
| Define | Identify that traffic congestion causes delays. |
| Ideate | Generate ideas such as alternative-route suggestions. |
| Prototype | Create a simple design of a traffic application. |
| Test | Show the design to users and collect feedback. |

## 6. Empathize Stage

### Definition

**Empathize** is the first stage of Design Thinking. It involves understanding the users, their needs, feelings, experiences and problems.

In simple words, it means putting yourself in another person's shoes to understand their situation.

### Ways to Understand Users

1. **Observation:** Watching users and understanding their behaviour.
2. **Interaction:** Talking to users and asking questions.
3. **Imagination:** Imagining how the user might feel in a particular situation.

### Example

Suppose a student is developing an application to help students select subjects after Class 10.

The student can:

- Interview Class 10 students.
- Talk to students already studying in Class 11.
- Understand their confusion and difficulties.
- Observe how they search for information about subjects.

This helps the developer understand the actual needs of the users.

## 7. Empathy Map

### Definition

An **Empathy Map** is a tool used to understand users' needs, behaviour, thoughts and feelings.

It helps us gain a deeper understanding of the problem from the user's point of view.

### What is a Persona?

A **Persona** is a realistic general representation of a user or a group of users.

A persona may include:

- Education
- Lifestyle
- Interests
- Values
- Goals
- Needs
- Thoughts
- Desires
- Attitudes
- Actions

### Four Quadrants of an Empathy Map

An Empathy Map is divided into four parts:

| Quadrant | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Says | What the user says aloud. |
| Thinks | What the user thinks about the problem. |
| Does | Actions performed by the user. |
| Feels | Emotional condition or feelings of the user. |

### Example: Empathy Map of a Student Choosing a Laptop

| Says | Thinks |
|---|---|
| "I need a laptop for my studies." | "Will this laptop be suitable for programming?" |
| "My budget is limited." | "Will it last for several years?" |

| Does | Feels |
|---|---|
| Searches for laptops online. | Confused about which laptop to buy. |
| Compares prices and specifications. | Worried about making the wrong choice. |

### Steps to Create an Empathy Map

1. Draw a grid divided into four quadrants.
2. Label the quadrants Says, Thinks, Does and Feels.
3. Collect observations by interviewing or observing users.
4. Write the observations in the appropriate quadrants.
5. Use sticky notes or write directly in the grid.

## 8. Define Stage

### Definition

The **Define Stage** is the stage in which information collected during the Empathize stage is analysed to identify and clearly state the actual problem.

It helps us prepare a clear, human-centred Problem Statement based on the user's unmet needs.

### Example

Problem: Students are confused while selecting subjects after Class 10.

After understanding the students' difficulties, we define the problem as:

"Students face difficulty in selecting subjects according to their interests while taking admission to Class 11."

### 5W1H Method

**Definition:** The 5W1H method is a questioning technique used to collect relevant facts and information about a problem.

It consists of six questions:

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Who? | Who is facing the problem? |
| What? | What is the problem? |
| When? | When does the problem occur? |
| Where? | Where does the problem occur? |
| Why? | Why does the problem occur? |
| How? | How can the problem be solved? |

### Example: Ashmitha's Traffic Problem

Ashmitha travels to her office every day. Her office is only 30 minutes away from her home, but due to traffic jams, it takes more than one hour.

The 5W1H method can be applied as follows:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who? | Ashmitha and other office commuters. |
| What? | Traffic jams cause delays. |
| When? | During busy office hours. |
| Where? | On roads between home and office. |
| Why? | Due to heavy traffic and other road-related issues. |
| How? | By suggesting alternative routes and improving traffic management. |

## 9. Ideate Stage

### Definition

**Ideation** is the process of generating different ideas and possible solutions to solve a particular problem.

In simple words, it means thinking of as many solutions as possible.

### Example

Problem: Students waste time searching for suitable subjects after Class 10.

Possible ideas:

1. Develop a mobile application.
2. Create a chatbot for subject suggestions.
3. Develop a website containing information about schools and subjects.
4. Create a system that analyses students' interests and suggests suitable subjects.

### Important Points About Ideation

- Generate as many ideas as possible.
- Encourage creativity and imagination.
- Do not criticise ideas during the initial brainstorming stage.
- Focus on quantity rather than quality of ideas initially.
- Evaluate and select the most feasible ideas later.

### Types of Ideation Techniques

#### A. Brainstorming

**Definition:** Brainstorming is a group activity in which participants freely share ideas and build on one another's suggestions to solve a problem.

**Example:**

Five students sit together and suggest different ways to reduce traffic jams.

Features:

- It is a group activity.
- Participants share ideas freely.
- Everyone can contribute.
- Ideas are not criticised during the session.
- A large number of ideas are collected.

#### B. Brain Dump

**Definition:** Brain Dump is an individual activity in which a person writes down all the ideas that come to mind.

**Example:**

A student independently writes ten ideas to reduce electricity consumption.

Features:

- It is performed individually.
- Ideas are written on paper or sticky notes.
- Ideas can be shared with the group later.

#### C. Brainwriting

**Definition:** Brainwriting is a technique in which participants write their ideas on paper and pass them to other participants, who add or improve those ideas.

The textbook also refers to Brainwriting as individual brainstorming.

**Example:**

1. Student A writes an idea on paper.
2. Student B receives the paper and adds another idea.
3. Student C improves the suggestion.
4. The papers are collected after the activity.
5. All ideas are discussed.

Features:

- Participants write their ideas.
- Papers are passed from one participant to another.
- Other participants add or improve suggestions.
- It gives less-confident or introverted members an opportunity to share ideas.

### Difference Between Ideation Techniques

| Brainstorming | Brain Dump | Brainwriting |
|---|---|---|
| Group activity. | Individual activity. | Participants write and pass ideas. |
| Ideas are shared verbally. | Ideas are written individually. | Ideas are written and improved by others. |
| Encourages group discussion. | Encourages independent thinking. | Encourages written participation. |

### Storyboarding

**Definition:** Storyboarding is a technique of presenting an idea through a sequence of drawings or pictures.

It helps explain how a proposed solution will work.

**Example:**

A student draws four pictures showing how a user opens a traffic application, enters a destination, receives an alternative route and reaches the destination.

## 10. Prototype Stage

### Definition

A **Prototype** is a simple model or sample of a proposed solution created to understand how the final product may look or work.

In simple words, it is an early version of a product created before developing the final product.

### Examples of Prototypes

- Simple drawings
- Posters
- Group role-playing
- Homemade gadgets
- 3D-printed products
- Basic application screen designs

### Characteristics of a Prototype

1. It should be quick and easy to develop.
2. It should be inexpensive.
3. It represents a basic form of the final product.
4. It helps answer questions about the proposed solution.
5. It helps collect useful feedback from users.
6. It can be created using everyday materials.

### Example

Suppose students want to develop a chatbot that suggests suitable subjects after Class 10.

Before developing the actual chatbot, they create a simple design showing:

- Welcome message.
- Questions about students' interests.
- Options for selecting interests.
- Suggested subjects.

This simple design is the prototype.

---

## 11. Test Stage

### Definition

**Testing** is the stage in which a prototype is shown to end users to collect feedback and understand whether the proposed solution is useful.

### Importance of Testing

1. Helps understand users' opinions.
2. Identifies problems in the prototype.
3. Provides an opportunity to improve the solution.
4. Helps understand whether the solution meets users' needs.
5. Provides another opportunity to interact with users and empathise with them.

### Example

A student creates a prototype of a subject-selection chatbot.

The student shows it to Class 10 students and asks:

- Is it easy to use?
- Are the suggested subjects useful?
- Are the questions clear?
- What improvements are required?

The feedback is used to improve the chatbot.

### Difference Between Prototype and Test

| Prototype | Test |
|---|---|
| Creates a sample model. | Checks the sample model. |
| Focuses on developing a basic solution. | Focuses on collecting feedback. |
| Example: Drawing an application screen. | Example: Showing the screen to users. |

**Note:** Design Thinking is an iterative process. Based on feedback, we may return to an earlier stage and improve the solution.

## 12. Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

### Definition

**Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)** are 17 global goals announced by the United Nations to achieve a better and more sustainable future for everyone.

### Sustainable Development

**Definition:** Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

In simple words, it means using resources responsibly so that future generations can also fulfil their needs.

**Example:**

Using solar energy instead of depending completely on non-renewable energy sources helps protect resources for the future.

### Importance of SDGs in Capstone Projects

- Students can identify problems related to SDGs.
- They can develop AI-based solutions for these problems.
- Their projects can contribute towards a sustainable future.
- They can work on real-world issues affecting society.

### Important SDGs and Their Examples

| SDG | Goal | Example of an AI Project |
|---|---|---|
| SDG 2 | Zero Hunger | Predicting food requirements to reduce food wastage. |
| SDG 3 | Good Health and Well-being | AI-based disease prediction. |
| SDG 4 | Quality Education | Personalised learning applications. |
| SDG 5 | Gender Equality | Identifying gender bias in recruitment data. |
| SDG 7 | Affordable and Clean Energy | Predicting electricity consumption. |
| SDG 11 | Sustainable Cities and Communities | AI-based traffic management. |
| SDG 12 | Responsible Consumption and Production | AI-based waste segregation. |

Other areas related to SDGs include:

- Combating hunger and poverty.
- Promoting renewable energy.
- Protecting natural resources.
- Providing access to education.
- Promoting responsible consumption and production.
- Developing safe and sustainable cities.

### Example: SDG 2 – Zero Hunger

The goal of SDG 2 is to achieve Zero Hunger.

Students can work on problems related to:

- Healthy food habits.
- Food supply to different areas.

- Seasonal food availability.

An AI-based system can predict food requirements and help reduce wastage.

## 13. Project Abstract Creation Using Design Thinking

### What is a Project Abstract?

A **Project Abstract** is a short description of a project that explains its name, problem, users, proposed ideas and solution-development approach.

It gives an overview of what the project is about.

### Format for Project Abstract Creation

The textbook provides the following format:

| No. | Section | Details |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project Name | A suitable name for the project. |
| 2 | Team Members | Names of students working on the project. |
| 3 | Problem Selection | Identify an important problem that may be solved using AI through surveys or interviews and align it with an SDG. |
| 4 | Users Affected | Identify the people who face the problem. |
| 5 | Empathize | Prepare an Empathy Map. |
| 6 | Define | Use 5W1H and write a Problem Statement. |
| 7 | Ideate | Brainstorm and list possible solutions. |
| 8 | Prototype | Create a drawing, poster, role-play, homemade gadget or 3D-printed model. |
| 9 | Test | Collect feedback. This step is optional at this stage. |

## 14. Textbook Example: Subject Selection System

### Project Overview

**Problem:** Students completing Class 10 are confused about which subjects to choose for their higher studies.

The project is aligned with **SDG 4 – Quality Education**.

### 1. Problem Selection

A survey among Class 10 students shows that many students are confused about selecting subjects for their Class 11 and 12 studies.

Students need guidance to choose subjects according to their interests and aptitude.

### 2. Users Affected

- Students who have passed Class 10.
- Students seeking admission to Class 11.
- Parents and well-wishers helping students with admission.

### 3. Empathize

The team can interview two categories of students:

1. Students already studying in Class 11 or 12 to understand the difficulties they faced while choosing subjects and schools.
2. Students who are going to take admission to understand their expectations and concerns.

The observations are organised into the four Empathy Map quadrants:

- Says
- Thinks
- Does
- Feels

### 4. Define Using 5W1H

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who? | Students seeking admission to Class 11 and their parents or well-wishers. |
| What? | Students cannot select subjects of their choice or find schools offering those subjects. |
| Where? | During the admission process at schools. |
| When? | During the admission period after Class 10. |
| Why? | Due to a lack of knowledge about available subjects and the curriculum. |
| How? | By developing a system that analyses students' aptitude and suggests suitable subjects and schools. |

### 5. Problem Statement

Students face difficulty in choosing subjects according to their interests while taking admission to Class 11. This problem can be solved by developing a system that analyses students' aptitude and suggests suitable subjects.

### 6. Ideate

The team suggests four possible solutions:

1. An application that accepts students' interests and suggests subjects.
2. A chatbot that interacts with students.
3. A robot that discusses options and provides advice.
4. A mobile application that collects information from different sources and provides results.

### 7. Prototype

The team selects the idea of developing a chatbot that helps students choose suitable subjects.

A simple prototype is prepared to demonstrate how the chatbot will interact with students.

### 8. Test

The prototype can be shown to students to collect feedback and identify improvements.
