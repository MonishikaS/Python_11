# C. Long Answer Questions

### Q1. Write down the steps of Problem Decomposition.

**Answer:**

**Definition:** Problem Decomposition is the process of breaking a complex problem into smaller and simpler parts so that it becomes easier to understand and solve.

The four steps of Problem Decomposition are:

**Step 1: Understand the Problem**

- Read and understand the problem carefully.
- Restate the problem in your own words.
- Identify the required inputs and outputs.
- Ask questions to clarify doubts.

**Step 2: Break the Problem into Large Parts**

- Divide the main problem into a few major sub-problems.
- Write down these parts separately.

**Step 3: Break Complicated Parts into Smaller Parts**

- Identify complicated sub-problems.
- Divide them further into smaller tasks.
- Continue until the tasks become simple.

**Step 4: Code One Small Part at a Time**

- Write code for each small part.
- Test each part separately.
- Identify and fix errors.
- Combine the parts to complete the solution.

**Example: Traffic Management Problem**

The main problem of traffic jams can be divided into:

1. Bad roads.
2. Accidents.
3. Office-hour traffic.
4. Rash driving.
5. Inappropriate traffic signals.
6. Overcrowded areas.

Rash driving can be further divided into overspeeding, inexperienced drivers and careless driving.

By solving each smaller problem separately, the overall traffic problem becomes easier to manage.

### Q2. Explain briefly the different stages of Design Thinking.

**Answer:**

Design Thinking is a user-centred approach used to understand problems and develop innovative solutions.

It consists of five stages:

**1. Empathize**

It is the first stage of Design Thinking. It involves understanding the users, their needs, feelings and problems.

**Example:** Interviewing students to understand their difficulties in selecting subjects after Class 10.

**2. Define**

In this stage, information collected during the Empathize stage is analysed to identify and clearly state the actual problem.

**Example:** Students are unable to select subjects according to their interests.

**3. Ideate**

It is the process of generating different ideas and possible solutions to solve the identified problem.

**Example:** Suggesting a mobile application, chatbot or website to help students select subjects.

**4. Prototype**

In this stage, a simple model or sample of the selected solution is created.

**Example:** Creating a basic chatbot design that suggests suitable subjects.

**5. Test**

In this stage, the prototype is shown to end users to collect feedback and identify improvements.

**Example:** Showing the chatbot prototype to students and collecting their opinions.

### Q3. Explain the different types of Ideation techniques.

**Answer:**

Ideation is the process of generating different ideas and possible solutions to solve a problem.

The three main ideation techniques explained in the chapter are:

**1. Brainstorming**

Brainstorming is a group activity in which participants freely share ideas and build on one another's suggestions.

Features:
- It is performed in a group.
- Participants share ideas freely.
- Ideas are not criticised during the session.
- A large number of ideas are collected.

**Example:** Five students discuss different ways to reduce traffic jams and suggest possible solutions.

**2. Brain Dump**

Brain Dump is an individual activity in which a person writes down all the ideas that come to mind.

Features:
- It is performed individually.
- Ideas are written on paper or sticky notes.
- Ideas can be shared with the group later.

**Example:** A student independently writes ten ideas to reduce electricity consumption.

**3. Brainwriting**

Brainwriting is a technique in which participants write their ideas on paper and pass them to others, who add or improve those ideas.

Features:
- Participants write their ideas individually.
- Papers are passed from one participant to another.
- Other participants add or improve suggestions.
- It helps less-confident members share their ideas.

**Example:**

1. Student A writes an idea.
2. Student B receives the paper and adds another idea.
3. Student C improves the suggestion.
4. Finally, all ideas are collected and discussed.

# D. Case Study

### Q1. Based on SDG-7, Affordable and Clean Energy, formulate a problem and write down the Design Thinking steps associated with it.

**Answer:**

**SDG 7 – Affordable and Clean Energy**

SDG 7 aims to ensure access to affordable, reliable, sustainable and modern energy for everyone.

**Problem:**

Many households consume excessive electricity, resulting in high electricity bills and wastage of energy.

An AI-based system can help households monitor electricity consumption and suggest ways to save energy.

### Design Thinking Steps

**1. Empathize**

Talk to people and understand their electricity consumption habits, difficulties and problems.

**2. Define**

Identify the main problem:

"Households are unable to monitor and control their electricity consumption effectively, resulting in high electricity bills."

**3. Ideate**

Generate different ideas to solve the problem:

- Develop an AI-based electricity monitoring system.
- Create an application to track electricity usage.
- Suggest ways to reduce electricity consumption.
- Predict monthly electricity bills.

**4. Prototype**

Create a simple model or application screen showing:

- Electricity consumption.
- Estimated monthly bill.
- Energy-saving suggestions.
- Daily electricity usage.

**5. Test**

Show the prototype to household users and collect their feedback.

Check whether the system is easy to use and whether the suggestions are helpful.

Improve the prototype based on the feedback.

**Conclusion:**

An AI-based electricity monitoring system can help households understand their electricity usage, reduce wastage and support SDG 7 – Affordable and Clean Energy.
